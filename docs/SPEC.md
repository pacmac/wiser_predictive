# wiser_predictive — SPEC v2 (DRAFT for approval, 2026-11-07)

Replaces SPEC v1 (in git history). v1 built a margin threshold ("heat needed when the reading is within 0.1 °C of the minimum") and called it predictive. Peter: that is an automation he can write in one line; it is a white elephant. This spec is what he asked for. Task: `predictive-integration`.

## GOAL
Predictive heating: use the inside and outside temperature AND THE RATE OF CHANGE of both, plus the weather forecast, to decide if and when to demand heat BEFORE a room falls so low that recovering it wastes energy (the fabric has cooled). It replaces Boost on Demand, which only stops short cycling.
It must be possible to LOOK at it: see a cold front coming in, see the room curves bend down faster, and see the action taken. Without that nobody can tell whether it is doing anything or whether the logic is right (Peter, 2026-11-07). **The visualisation is a first-class deliverable, not a later nicety. A stage is not done until its chart has been looked at.**

## HOW THE HEATING WORKS (facts Peter gave; verify before relying on them)
- Wiser rooms are mostly PASSIVE: they take free heat whenever water is circulating and the room is below its HIGH. One room (here the lounge) is ACTIVE and is the one that demands heat. Not every room can be passive; someone has to call.
- Whether a passive room below its LOW calls for heat by itself: ANSWERED FROM THE CODE 2026-11-09 (Peter: "that will be in the code"), see STAGE 3 DESIGN, FACTS. It does, by the hub's ordinary thermostat acting on the passive room's real setpoint (its LOW, 12.0 C here). Still never SEEN here: no passive room came within 0.7 °C of its LOW in 8 days. The predictor still does not depend on it: the active room is the lever.
- Boost on Demand exists to stop short cycling: the thermostats sit on the valves, warm up quickly, and the firmware gives no usable hysteresis. The predictor must therefore decide WHEN to start a heat call and HOW LONG to hold it (long runs, no cycling), so it replaces that job too.
- The room reading comes from valve-mounted iTRVs and is biased by the radiator: the lounge's two iTRVs read 17.9 and 19.1 at the same moment; the room figure (18.5) is a mix. A reading taken while the radiator is hot is not the room air. The model must learn cooling only from periods when the valves are closed, and treat heat-on readings separately. A separate air sensor away from the radiator would be better; that is Peter's hardware decision.
- Wiser publishes per-room and per-channel heating demand (%, the "LTS" sensors). The predictor's own demand is expressed the same way so the two can be drawn on one axis and compared.

## WHAT IT DECIDES
Per room, continuously: given the room's measured temperature and rate, the outside temperature and its forecast ramp, how long until the room reaches its minimum if nothing is done (`time_to_minimum`), how long a heat call needs to take effect and to recover (`lead`: dead time + recovery), and therefore the latest safe moment to demand heat. Across rooms: demand heat when the earliest room says so; the others take the free heat. Output is a planned demand over the next 24 h, not just a yes/no now.

## MODEL
Per room, first order (RC): `dT/dt = (T_out(t) − T) / τ + g·u(t)`, with `u` the heat supplied (0 with the valves closed). `T_out(t)` is the measured outside temperature now and the hourly forecast ahead, so a cold front (a steep falling ramp) shortens `time_to_minimum`. Learned per room from history:
- `τ`, the cooling time constant: from valves-closed periods only.
- `g` and the dead time: from heat-up periods (boosts and boiler runs).
- the sensor's radiator bias while heating (see above).
Every number is fitted or derived from the data, and the derivation is recorded beside it. No default entity ids, no house values in code.
The minimum for a passive room is its LOW. The active room has no LOW: its minimum is a number the user sets in the options form (no default), because its target follows the schedule (lounge: 19.0, then 17.0, overnight 12.5–13.0).
The v1 cooling fits (`predict_linear`, `predict_newton`) were a weak test: one outside value, a short window, a biased sensor. They are a starting point only.
BUILT (stage 2a, `thermal.py`, measured by `replay.py --model data`): the cooling part. `dT/dt = c0 + c1*T + c2*Tout`, learned per room from windows of free cooling (no heating, and not in the 60 min after it, because the valve sensors read the warm radiator). Trained on the first 6 of 10 days, scored on the last 4 against "the temperature stays where it is" and against the same model without the outside term. Results (average error at 6 h ahead, measured outside used as a perfect forecast): lounge 0.23 against 0.33; bed 1 0.24 against 0.36; bed 2 0.26 against 0.45; bed 3 0.20 against 0.40; bath 0.30 against 0.42; kitchen 0.27 against 0.29 (no better). Without the outside term the model is worse than "stays" at 12 h. Cooling time constants 15-33 h; the lounge settles at 9.9 / 12.0 / 14.2 °C when it is 5 / 8 / 11 °C outside, below its 12.5 °C floor, so it reaches its floor first and is the room to protect. Derived: the 60 min settle (the fitted constant stops changing from 30 min), one day of windows needed before it will fit (the outside swings once a day), 8 min heat reaction (measured: after a heat call the lounge fell no further than one 0.1 °C step, and the first +0.1 °C took 0.5-7.2 min over 7 boosts). The active room has no LOW setting; its floor is the lowest target it held for an hour or more in the history (a boost lasts 14-15 min). Limits: 10 days, one autumn; the forecast is used as the outside input in the panel but was scored with measured outside; heat-up and the best time to heat are NOT modelled yet (stage 2b).
HORIZON: the prediction runs as far as the forecast goes (the hourly forecast is 48 h), not a fixed 24 h; a 24 h limit hid a breach about 41 h out (Peter, 2026-11-07: "how many hours in the future do we predict"). With no forecast it falls back to 24 h. The card says where it ends and whether the room is still falling then.

ROOM CHARACTER (built, shown on the panel as "What each room is like"; Peter, 2026-11-07: use the published name and calculation). Published names: THERMAL TIME CONSTANT tau = total thermal capacitance / total heat loss coefficient (C/HTC), the time to cover 63.2% of a step change; the HEAT TRANSFER (HEAT LOSS) COEFFICIENT, HTC, in W/K, is measured in the UK by the co-heating test and needs the heating power in watts, which this system does not have (it knows demand in percent), so only the ratio tau is observable here, not C and HTC separately; TIME LAG and DECREMENT FACTOR describe how a daily outside swing arrives inside. "Outdoor coupling" (settled degrees of room per degree of outside) is our own label; below 1 it means heat is also exchanged with other rooms or gained inside, which a one-node RC model cannot separate (published models use several nodes). Shown per room with a leave-one-day-out uncertainty. FINDING with 10 days: tau is 19-26 h but moves by +-7-11 h when any one day is left out (kitchen +-3 h), so the differences between rooms are NOT yet real; the daily-swing delay (about 5.3 h in every room) is solid because it barely depends on tau once tau is long.
STAGE 2c (built; Peter, 2026-11-07: a constantly averaging / adjusting value that becomes more refined as time elapses): the model is refined continuously, as the published online methods do (recursive least squares with a forgetting factor; Kalman filters). The fit is linear, so it depends on the data only through sums (the normal equations); `learning.py` reduces each completed day to a few sums per room, `learner.py` keeps them in Home Assistant's storage (`.storage/wiser_predictive.learned`) so the memory outlives the recorder's 10 days, and every figure on the panel (the thermal character, the prediction and the plan) is computed from the sums of ALL days kept. Summing the days gives exactly the fit on all the data (tested). A day is kept once it is over; a partly-available day at the old edge of the recorder never replaces a fuller stored one. It is updated when the panel is opened and every 6 hours in the background (a day is kept within 6 hours of ending, against a 10-day retention). Forgetting: old days fade with a half-life so the model follows the seasons and changes to the room (a window left open, new furnishings). The half-life is CHOSEN FROM THE DATA, not set: once 14 days are kept, each recent day is predicted from the days before it with each candidate half-life (7, 14, 30, 60 days, or none) and the best is used; until then there is no forgetting. That candidate list is a search grid. The uncertainty (leave one day out) narrows as days are added (tested on a synthetic room). Limit: it learns only from days kept from now on plus the recorder's last 10; a season of history cannot be recovered.

The plan now draws the room rising over the radiator's lag and checks that it never reaches the minimum before the heat has overtaken the cooling (the call moves earlier if it would). MOISTURE FLOOR (replaces the fixed 12.5 C minimum; estimated, because no sensor exists).
- Limits. Surface relative humidity (RH) of about 80% for a month or more is enough for common moulds to grow, and the air needs a lower RH than that because a surface is colder than the air (Oreszczyn and Pretlove, Condensation Targeter II: https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/128567). APPROVED DOCUMENT F, PRIMARY TEXT (read 9 Nov from https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/61deba42d3bf7f054fcc243d/ADF1.pdf, Volume 1 dwellings, 2021 edition, Appendix B, document page 41): B10, for ALL dwellings, new and existing: the moisture criteria are likely to be met if, during the colder months, the moving-average surface water activity of the internal surfaces of EXTERNAL WALLS is always below Table B2: 0.75 over 1 month, 0.85 over 1 week, 0.95 over 1 day. B11, for NEW dwellings only: the criteria are likely met if the moving-average indoor AIR relative humidity is always below Table B3: 65% (1 month), 75% (1 week), 85% (1 day). So the 65/75/85 figures (which UCL's summary, https://www.ucl.ac.uk/bartlett/environmental-design/relative-humidity-dwellings, gave) are the new-build air figures; for an existing house the test is the wall-surface water activity (surface RH / 100), which depends on a wall temperature nobody measures. Conversion, with es rising 6.6% per K (fact above): air limit = aw x e^(-0.066 d) for a surface d K colder than the air. Monthly 0.75: d=1 gives 70.2%, d=2 65.7%, d=3 61.5%, d=4 57.6%; daily 0.95: d=2 83.2%, d=3 77.9%. Table B3's 65% against B2's 0.75 is a surface 2.2 K colder (ln(0.75/0.65) / 0.066 = 2.17). d = (1 - f) x (T_in - T_out) with f the wall's temperature factor, so with a 5.5 K difference (12.5 against 7.0) and f from 0.5 to 0.75, d is 1.4 to 2.75 K and the monthly air limit is 63 to 68% (0.75 x e^(-0.066 x 2.75) = 62.6%, 0.75 x e^(-0.066 x 1.375) = 68.5%): 65% stays the right central figure, and the wall's f is the unmeasured uncertainty (the daily and weekly figures move with it too). The criterion applies in the colder months, as a moving average (a running mean, not an instantaneous reading). UK guidance on the air TEMPERATURE of a vacant house is 10-15 C (insurers' requirements, https://homebuilding.co.uk/advice/minimum-house-temperature) and for occupied homes 14 C at the very least up to 18 C; none of it is mould science. Nothing found supports or refutes Peter's 12 C.

In an empty, ventilated house the room's water vapour pressure is about the outdoor air's. e_out = es(T_out) x RH_out; RH_room = e_out / es(T_room); es(T) = 6.112 exp(17.62 T / (243.12 + T)) hPa (Magnus). The floor for a limit L is the room temperature at which es(T_room) = e_out / L. ## THE FALL AFTER A BOOST, MEASURED (2026-11-12)
- Defect found: the plain cooling model (one fit over all free-cooling windows, mostly quiet ones) falls too slowly in the hours after a boost. Lounge, 10 boosts in 10 days, from SETTLED_S (60 min) after each run: at 3 h the model falls 0.5-0.6 of what was read in 9 of 10 boosts (median 0.62 C against 1.10 C), at 6 h 0.6-1.1 (median 1.07 against 1.50). Near the floor it matches (0.09 C/h at 12.9 C, 5.9 C outside). So the plan drew the room 0.3-0.5 C too warm for hours after a run and held it too long.
- Fix: `thermal.fit_after_run` fits, from the room's own boosts, a drop D and an arrival time constant tau so that the room is D * (1 - exp(-t / tau)) lower than the cooling model, t from the settle point. `Heating.drop` / `drop_tau_h` carry it; `plan` subtracts it from the cooling tail after every run; `series.predict` fits and attaches it. Lounge: D = 0.33 C, tau = 1 h. Leave-one-boost-out, the room's error fell from 0.35 C to 0.20 C. With it the 46 h plan keeps the same call times and shortens each hold by 2 to 3.5 h.
- Not known: whether the extra fall is the room or the valve-mounted sensor cooling (no air sensor in the lounge). Either way the sensor is what the cold-room trigger reads, so the plan follows the sensor.
- Never below 0: a room that falls slower than the model is left to the model.
- Latent, not fixed: `thermal._linear` holds the last outside value for ever past the end of the series. A preview run on stale outside data (series ending three days earlier) silently fitted on a constant outside temperature. The live integration reads the recorder fresh, so it is not known to be hit.
